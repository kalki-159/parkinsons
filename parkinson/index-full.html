About the Disease

What is Parkinson's Disease?

A progressive neurological disorder affecting movement, arising from the loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the brain.

Neurological Origin

Parkinson's occurs when neurons in the substantia nigra progressively deteriorate. These neurons produce dopamine — a chemical messenger that coordinates smooth, controlled movement.

Progressive Condition

Symptoms worsen gradually over time. Early signs include a barely noticeable tremor in one hand, while later stages affect balance, speech, and cognitive functions. It is the second most common neurodegenerative disease.

Core Symptoms

The hallmark signs are tremors, bradykinesia (slowness of movement), rigidity, and postural instability. Non-motor symptoms include depression, sleep disorders, and cognitive changes.

Global Impact

Over 10 million people worldwide live with Parkinson's disease. Men are 1.5× more likely to develop it. Most diagnoses occur after age 60, though early-onset cases exist.

Cures & Treatments

Managing Parkinson's Today

While there is no cure yet, a combination of therapies significantly improves quality of life.

Levodopa / Carbidopa
The gold-standard medication that replenishes dopamine levels in the brain, reducing motor symptoms effectively.
Deep Brain Stimulation
Surgical implant delivers electrical pulses to specific brain regions, reducing tremors and rigidity significantly.
Physical Therapy
Targeted exercises improve balance, flexibility, gait, and overall motor function significantly.
Stem Cell Research
Emerging therapies aim to replace lost neurons, with promising early clinical trial results worldwide.

Understanding Parkinson's Disease

A concise guide to the disease, its progression, treatments, and how CogniVue aims to transform early detection.

What is Parkinson's Disease?

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a chronic, progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting the motor system. It results from the gradual degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra — a brain region critical for coordinated movement. As neurons die, dopamine levels drop, disrupting the circuits that regulate motion.

Disease Progression

Parkinson's progresses through five Hoehn & Yahr stages, each representing a distinct level of symptom severity.

Stage 1
Unilateral Disease
Symptoms appear on one side only. Tremor or rigidity is mild. Daily activities are unaffected. Often missed or misattributed to stress.
Stage 2
Bilateral Involvement
Symptoms affect both sides. Posture and gait may change. Daily tasks take longer but remain manageable. Balance is still maintained.
Stage 3
Mild to Moderate Bilateral
Balance impairment becomes notable. Still functionally independent but significantly slowed. This stage benefits most from early intervention.
Stage 4
Severe Disability
Capable of standing with assistance. Daily activities severely limited. Caregiver support becomes essential.
Stage 5
Wheelchair-Bound or Bedridden
Requires constant nursing care. Severe cognitive and motor deficits. Full-time caregiver assistance required.

Treatments & Therapies

While no cure exists yet, modern treatment strategies can dramatically improve quality of life and maintain independence for longer.

Pharmacological Treatments
  • Levodopa/Carbidopa — gold standard
  • Dopamine agonists (Pramipexole, Ropinirole)
  • MAO-B inhibitors (Selegiline, Rasagiline)
  • COMT inhibitors to extend levodopa effect
  • Amantadine for dyskinesia management
Surgical Interventions
  • Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)
  • Focused Ultrasound thalamotomy for tremor
  • Pallidotomy for dyskinesias
  • Gene therapy trials (AAV-based)
Rehabilitative Therapies
  • Physiotherapy for gait and balance
  • LSVT LOUD speech therapy
  • Occupational therapy for daily living
  • Dance therapy (tango) for motor coordination
Emerging Research
  • Mediterranean diet — neuroprotective evidence
  • Aerobic exercise — slows motor decline
  • Alpha-synuclein antibody therapies
  • Stem cell neuronal replacement research

How CogniVue Works

A technical overview of our AI-powered Parkinson's screening system.

🎯 The Problem We Solve

Parkinson's is difficult to diagnose early. Traditional diagnosis relies on clinical evaluation — subjective, expensive, and inaccessible. Up to 75% of PD cases are misdiagnosed in early stages. CogniVue bridges this gap with an accessible video-based screening tool that flags potential motor abnormalities before they become severe.

📹 Video Capture & Processing

Users record a 5–15 second clip via webcam or upload pre-recorded footage. The video is sent to the backend as multipart/form-data via POST to /predict-video.

🔬 Frame Estimation & Classification

Frames are extracted and passed through a pose estimation model (MediaPipe / OpenPose) to generate skeletal keypoints. Features like tremor frequency (Hz), amplitude (mm), and rhythm consistency (%) are fed into a trained classifier (LSTM / 3D-CNN) that outputs a 0–100% risk score with severity label.

📊 Result Interpretation

The dashboard displays the risk score, confidence level, extracted metrics, and a tremor frequency waveform. Severity suggestions guide next steps: Normal (<30%), Mild (30–60%), or Severe (>60%). All results include a medical disclaimer — this is a screening tool, not a diagnostic instrument.
